Assignment Questions

Part 1From Raw Data to Frequencies

Select one categorical and one quantitative variable.

Categorical variable

Report categories, frequencies, and percentages. What does the distribution tell you? Use at least one frequency or percentage as evidence.

Quantitative variable

  1. Where are observations most concentrated?
  2. Are any intervals unusually common or uncommon?
  3. What can you see that was difficult to recognize in the raw data?

Use specific frequencies, percentages, or intervals as evidence.

Part 2Build the Visual

Select three variables representing different types of data. For each:

  1. Choose and generate a graph.
  2. Identify the variable and graph.
  3. Explain why the graph is appropriate.
  4. Describe the most important information it communicates.
Part 3Read the Distribution

Select one quantitative variable and examine its distribution. Consider concentration, symmetry or skew, gaps, clusters, unusually high or low observations, and surprises.

Support your interpretation with specific evidence. What can the shape reveal that a single descriptive statistic might miss?

Part 4Change the Picture

For the same quantitative variable, create two histograms using different numbers of classes.

  1. What changed in the appearance?
  2. What became easier or harder to see?
  3. Did the underlying data change?
  4. Which version is more useful, and why?
  5. How can display decisions influence interpretation?
Part 5Visual Judgment

Select one graph to present to organizational leadership.

  1. What important finding does it show? Use specific evidence.
  2. Why does this graph communicate effectively?
  3. What might someone incorrectly conclude, and why is the graph insufficient for that conclusion?
  4. What additional information or analysis would help?
What to submit

Submit one document in Canvas with Parts 1–5. Include your Case Code and Verification Code and the requested frequency information and graphs.
